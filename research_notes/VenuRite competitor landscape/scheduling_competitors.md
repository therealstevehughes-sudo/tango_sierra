# Workforce Scheduling / Rota Competitors: Deputy, When I Work, Fourth/HotSchedules

## What are the core scheduling features of each product, and what do they do noticeably better than a typical implementation?

### Takeaway
All three are scheduling-first, mobile-native platforms with the same core building blocks (drag-and-drop rota builder, shift swap/open-shift marketplace, availability management, mobile clock-in, payroll/POS integrations), but they differentiate on depth: Deputy leans on AI auto-scheduling plus broad compliance/labor-law tooling across geographies; When I Work is the leanest/cheapest with a strong team-communication layer; Fourth/HotSchedules is the restaurant-industry specialist with the deepest demand-forecasting and labor-optimization engine, built on 20+ years of hospitality data.

### Cited Findings
- **Deputy** — Smart/AI Auto-Scheduling generates schedules "based on historical patterns, predefined structures, or by filling unassigned shifts," using AI to meet requirements for budget, breaks, training, and spread of hours — [Deputy Scheduling Software](https://www.deputy.com/features/scheduling-software)
- **Deputy** — Shift swap flow: team members can request swaps, pick up open shifts, or give away shifts in-app; managers approve/decline with one click, "reducing back-and-forth communication" — [Deputy Features](https://www.deputy.com/features)
- **Deputy** — Geofencing: managers can block clock-in away from the work location; minimum geofence radius 100m/100yd, maximum 1km/1000yd; geofence blocks *ending* a shift away from location but does not block clocking in/out of breaks away from the site — [Deputy Help Center](https://help.deputy.com/hc/en-au/articles/4692721789583-How-can-I-ensure-my-team-members-are-clocking-in-for-work-at-the-correct-location), [Enable Geofence](https://help.deputy.com/hc/en-au/articles/4657686206095-Enable-Geofence-in-Deputy)
- **Deputy** — Break/labor compliance: "automated meal break reminders, missed-break flags, and real-time overtime alerts to help multi-location teams work toward compliance with federal, state, and local regulations"; time clock "alerts managers when breaks are missed altogether" — [Deputy Time Clock](https://www.deputy.com/features/employee-time-clock)
- **Deputy** — G2 reviewer sentiment: "Deputy excels in user-friendliness, with many users highlighting its intuitive interface that simplifies tasks like clocking in and out, tracking shifts, and managing timesheets"; "demand-based forecasting is a genuine strength, and its mobile app is well-regarded for shift swaps and clock-ins" — [G2 Deputy vs HotSchedules](https://www.g2.com/compare/deputy-vs-hotschedules)
- **When I Work** — Combines scheduling, time tracking, and team messaging; features include auto-scheduling, shift swapping, GPS/photo clock-ins, overtime alerts, and labor forecasting — [Business.com](https://www.business.com/reviews/when-i-work/)
- **When I Work** — Employees can update availability, request time off, swap shifts, and claim open shifts directly from mobile; managers get notifications for swap/time-off requests — [BusinessNewsDaily](https://www.businessnewsdaily.com/when-i-work.html)
- **When I Work** — Geofenced GPS time clock syncs with pay rules and scheduling; "provides automated alerts to prevent overtime and labor compliance problems"; GPS clock-ins ensure employees only clock in at the designated job site — [When I Work: Geofence Time Clock](https://wheniwork.com/blog/geofence-time-clock)
- **When I Work** — Comparison sourcing notes its "clear coverage workflow that reduces manual chasing when coverage changes happen," but its overall feature set is "narrower than Deputy or HotSchedules" — [SaaS Battle](https://trysaasbattle.com/deputy-vs-when-i-work/)
- **Fourth/HotSchedules** — AI-enabled labor forecasting "can predict demand within 5% accuracy to actual experience" and "improves forecasting accuracy by up to 75% by leveraging more than 20 years of industry-specific data" — [Fourth: Employee Scheduling & Labor Forecasting](https://www.fourth.com/employee-scheduling-labor-forecasting/)
- **Fourth/HotSchedules** — Labor Optimization (formerly Activity Based Forecasting) is an add-on letting operators set scheduling thresholds based on projected guest counts/sales — [HS Labor Optimization overview](https://help.hotschedules.com/hc/en-us/articles/360031516871-HS-Labor-Optimization-formerly-Activity-Based-Forecasting-Introduction-and-Overview)
- **Fourth/HotSchedules** — Three-step automated scheduling: labor rules configured at implementation → AI forecasting generates demand + auto-creates house shifts → Auto Schedule assigns staff by availability, skill level, and labor law, "producing an optimized, compliant schedule in seconds" — [Fourth: Workforce Management Software](https://www.fourth.com/solution/workforce-management-software)
- **Fourth/HotSchedules** — "Schedule Score": a color-coded visual quality indicator showing how well a draft schedule aligns with forecast, overtime risk, availability conflicts, and labor rule compliance before it's published — [Fourth: Schedule Scoring](https://www.fourth.com/solution/workforce-management-software/schedule-score)
- **Fourth/HotSchedules** — Vendor claim: accurate forecasting lets managers "save up to 5% in labor costs" — [Fourth: Employee Scheduling & Labor Forecasting](https://www.fourth.com/employee-scheduling-labor-forecasting/)
- Comparative ratings (aggregator scoring, not raw review scores): When I Work 89%, Deputy 91%, HotSchedules 87%; Deputy 4.6/5 (662 reviews) vs. HotSchedules 4.3/5 (104 reviews) on the cited aggregator — [Crozdesk-sourced comparison summary](https://crozdesk.com/compare/wheniwork-vs-hotschedules-vs-deputy-8b5f4d88-a8ba-4131-97ca-f3c070ef174f)

### Inferences
- Deputy and HotSchedules both now market "AI auto-scheduling," but HotSchedules' version is explicitly tied to a hospitality-specific demand-forecasting dataset (20+ years), which is a defensible moat Deputy's more horizontal (multi-industry) forecasting likely can't match for restaurants/hospitality specifically.
- When I Work's narrower feature set and lower price point position it as the "good enough" budget option for smaller operators, while Deputy and HotSchedules compete more on depth (compliance breadth for Deputy, forecasting depth for HotSchedules) — this is the gap VenuRite should note when positioning: neither budget nor enterprise players bundle compliance-document/task management in the same product.
- Geofencing implementations across Deputy and When I Work are functionally similar (GPS radius-based clock-in blocking); this is treated as table stakes, not a differentiator, in this product category — VenuRite should assume it needs geofencing just to be competitive, not to lead.

### Gaps
- No independent (non-vendor) verification was found for Fourth's "5% accuracy" and "75% forecasting improvement" and "5% labor cost savings" claims — these are vendor marketing statistics from fourth.com, not corroborated by third-party benchmarks or reviews. Flagged as vendor claims only.
- Could not find specific detail on Deputy's or When I Work's UK/EU Working Time Regulations handling (e.g., 48-hour opt-out tracking, 11-hour rest period flags) beyond general labor-compliance marketing language; most sourced material is US-labor-law-centric (overtime, meal breaks).

## What do real reviews (Capterra, G2, Trustpilot, App Store/Google Play) say — praise and complaints — for each product?

### Takeaway
Across all three, reviewers consistently praise ease of use, mobile shift-swap/clock-in convenience, and time savings on rota-building; the dominant complaints are inconsistent/slow customer support, pricing changes or hidden fees, and app stability/bugs after updates — with a notable gap between higher scores on incentivized review platforms (Capterra/G2) and lower, more critical scores on independent platforms (Trustpilot).

### Cited Findings
- **Deputy** — Rated 4.6/5 on Capterra/G2, but only 3.9/5 on Trustpilot Australia based on verified customer reviews — the source cited explicitly frames this as "a notable difference between incentivized and independent reviews" — [aggregator-sourced comparison](https://www.capterra.com/p/167811/Deputy/reviews/)
- **Deputy praise** — Users describe offering and swapping shifts as "a breeze"; students say it's easy to update availability or swap shifts via the app and check schedules; mobile app commended for easy time clock and communications — [App Store reviews summary](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/deputy-shift-schedule-maker/id477070330?see-all=reviews)
- **Deputy complaints — support** — Trustpilot Jan 2025: "The support is extremely disappointing. Not only is the response time painfully slow, but the responses completely ignore the details of my requests." Dec 2025: "ZERO answers, ZERO service, ZERO support, and ZERO progress for the last 4-5 months." Another: "We have tried to contact deputy and their customer support for more than 6 months. Their system does not allow to submit a ticket." — [Trustpilot: deputy.com](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/deputy.com)
- **Deputy praise — support (contrasting)** — Feb 2025 Trustpilot review: "Deputy TM Solutions was prompt knowledgeable and very competent. At every stage they surpassed my expectations." Other sources note a "significant portion of users praise Deputy's customer support for being fast, responsive, and available 24/7... especially during onboarding and troubleshooting." — [Trustpilot: deputy.com](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/deputy.com)
- **Deputy complaints — support (specific quote)** — "the customer services is absolutely disgraceful these days, you cant get support for account finance issues during weekends" — [aggregator-sourced review summary, Capterra-based](https://www.capterra.com/p/167811/Deputy/reviews/)
- **Deputy complaints — app stability** — "Recent updates have introduced significant performance issues with many users reporting slowness, frequent glitches, freezing, and loading problems"; mobile app "isn't as functional as the desktop version and... lags from time to time" — [App Store / review summary](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/deputy-shift-schedule-maker/id477070330?see-all=reviews)
- **Deputy complaints — pricing** — "Deputy's pricing can be misleading and not entirely transparent, with reports of issues with unexpected charges, such as fees for potential employees who do not complete onboarding" — [Capterra-sourced review summary](https://www.capterra.com/p/167811/Deputy/reviews/)
- **Deputy complaints — integrations** — Some users report integration glitches and data sync issues connecting Deputy with third-party software — [review summary](https://www.capterra.com/p/167811/Deputy/reviews/)
- **When I Work** — G2 reviewers note per-user fees "compound quickly for larger teams," with "a few small business owners mention switching to free scheduling alternatives due to cost"; one reviewer flagged pricing concerns specific to Canadian businesses — [G2: When I Work Reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/when-i-work/reviews)
- **When I Work** — Despite pricing gripes, G2 reviews suggest "the time savings in scheduling, payroll integration, and attendance tracking justify the cost for most small and mid-market teams" — [G2: When I Work Reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/when-i-work/reviews)
- **When I Work complaints — accuracy** — Comparative source notes "When I Work users report occasional inaccuracies in time tracking and total hour calculations" — [comparison source](https://crozdesk.com/compare/wheniwork-vs-hotschedules-vs-deputy-8b5f4d88-a8ba-4131-97ca-f3c070ef174f)
- **HotSchedules complaints — app bugs** — "Most users report bugs and issues cause slow loading, app crashes, login problems, and unreliable real-time updates"; specifically, "the Android app constantly fails to login with constant exceptions thrown so they have to always restart to see their schedule" — [Google Play / TrustRadius-sourced summary](https://www.trustradius.com/products/hotschedules/reviews)
- **HotSchedules complaints — support** — "Customer support is a major complaint... very difficult to get responses... will take a day and up to 3 days to hear anything back"; some say "it can be very hard to get ahold of someone and it takes days if not weeks for service tickets to be received" — [Capterra/TrustRadius-sourced summary](https://www.capterra.com/p/52611/HotSchedules/reviews/)
- **HotSchedules complaints — feature gaps/UI** — "The app lacks vital features, such as time-tracking functionality, and has a dated web app UI and unresponsive customer support"; "the app and website are not as intuitive and streamlined as desired" — [review summary](https://www.softwareadvice.com/hr/hotschedules-profile/reviews/)
- **HotSchedules praise** — "Some customers praise customer support for being quick to act and responsive to issues"; "most users report restaurant use simplifies shift management, saves time, and supports frequent schedule changes" — [Capterra/Software Advice-sourced summary](https://www.capterra.com/p/52611/HotSchedules/reviews/)
- **HotSchedules — mobile app cost complaint** — Mobile app users must pay a $2.99 one-time fee to download HotSchedules from Google Play or the Apple App Store — [HotSchedules cost summary](https://www.hotschedulessignup.com/hotschedules-cost/)

### Inferences
- The Capterra/G2-vs-Trustpilot rating gap for Deputy (4.6 vs 3.9) suggests VenuRite should weight independent review sites more heavily when benchmarking — incentivized review platforms likely oversample satisfied "solicited-after-onboarding" respondents and undersample long-term support friction.
- Customer support responsiveness is the single most consistent pain point across all three vendors (Deputy, When I Work implicitly via cost-driven churn, and HotSchedules explicitly) — this is a repeatable competitive opening for VenuRite if it can staff genuinely fast support at a smaller scale.
- Post-update app instability (Deputy) and login/crash issues (HotSchedules) point to a broader category weakness: mature scheduling apps accumulate feature bloat and regress on core mobile reliability — VenuRite (as a newer, more focused Flutter app) should treat clock-in/shift-swap reliability as a core quality bar to beat, not just a nice-to-have.

### Gaps
- Could not source direct pull-quotes specifically from Google Play (as distinct from App Store/aggregator summaries) for any of the three products; searches returned only aggregator-summarized sentiment, not verbatim Play Store reviews.
- Could not find independent, dated Trustpilot rating figures for When I Work or HotSchedules specifically (only for Deputy); when I Work and HotSchedules Trustpilot pages were referenced in search results but not fetched for a specific score.
- No explicit review evidence was found of operators complaining about *juggling a separate compliance app alongside* Deputy/When I Work/HotSchedules specifically — general industry commentary confirms restaurant operators dislike disconnected tool stacks (see next section) but no reviewer was found saying "I use HotSchedules for rota and a separate app for compliance/HACCP and hate switching between them." This specific angle is a gap, not a confirmed finding.

## Pricing models — and complaints about pricing changes / value for money

### Takeaway
Deputy and When I Work both use transparent per-user/month tiered pricing (Deputy ~$5–$9/user/month across Lite/Core/Pro as of an October 2025 repricing, plus a $30/month minimum spend; When I Work $2.50–$8/user/month across three tiers), while Fourth/HotSchedules does not publish pricing at all and requires a sales quote — third-party estimates put it around $40–$49/month per location or roughly $2/user/month for smaller teams.

### Cited Findings
- **Deputy** — In October 2025, Deputy restructured to a 3-plan lineup: Lite ($5/user/month), Core ($6.50/user/month), Pro ($9/user/month), with a 10% discount for annual billing; HR and Analytics+ add-ons cost $2 and $1.50/user respectively; a minimum monthly spend of $30/month per invoice applies to monthly Lite/Core/Pro plans as of September 1, 2025 — [Deputy Pricing](https://www.deputy.com/pricing)
- **Deputy** — A separate source cites Deputy pricing "starting at $1.50 per user per month," conflicting with the $5 Lite-tier figure above; this discrepancy likely reflects an older legacy pricing page vs. the October 2025 repricing, and was not reconciled — [conflicting figure noted](https://connecteam.com/reviews/deputy/) vs. [Deputy Pricing](https://www.deputy.com/pricing)
- **When I Work** — Three tiers: Essentials $2.50/user/month (core scheduling, messaging, OpenShifts, payroll/POS integrations), Pro $5/user/month (advanced scheduling rules, role permissions, custom reporting), Premium $8/user/month (API access, webhooks, SAML/SSO); 14-day free trial, no credit card required — [When I Work Pricing](https://wheniwork.com/pricing)
- **HotSchedules/Fourth** — Pricing is not published; third-party estimate: roughly $2/user/month (a 25-person team ≈ $50/month), though "enterprise pricing varies" and requires a sales quote — [SelectHub-sourced estimate](https://www.selecthub.com/p/employee-scheduling-software/hotschedules/)
- **HotSchedules/Fourth** — Other third-party estimates put starting cost at $40/month per location, rising to $49/month for some locations — [SaaSWorthy-sourced estimate](https://www.saasworthy.com/product/hotschedules-powered-by-fourth/pricing)
- **HotSchedules/Fourth** — Additional mobile app download fee: $2.99 one-time charge on Google Play/Apple App Store — [HotSchedules cost breakdown](https://www.hotschedulessignup.com/hotschedules-cost/)

### Inferences
- HotSchedules' opaque, quote-only pricing (plus a $2.99 mobile download fee) is itself a friction point relative to Deputy and When I Work's transparent self-serve pricing pages — this is a value-for-money complaint vector even before considering per-seat cost, and a place where VenuRite's pricing transparency could be marketed as an advantage.
- Deputy's October 2025 repricing plus new $30/month minimum spend is recent enough that broad review sentiment specifically about *this* pricing change had not yet propagated into search-indexed reviews at the time of this research (Sept 2026) — worth flagging as an area to re-check closer to publication, since pricing-model changes are a classic trigger for review-site backlash.

### Gaps
- No direct customer complaints were found specifically reacting to Deputy's October 2025 repricing (too recent relative to indexed review content, or not surfaced by search).
- Could not find a reconciled, current, single-sourced HotSchedules price list; all figures are third-party estimates, not from fourth.com directly (fourth.com does not publish pricing).

## What features connect scheduling to compliance/training-record tracking, and is there evidence of "juggling multiple apps" frustration relevant to VenuRite's compliance+scheduling combination thesis?

### Takeaway
HotSchedules is the only one of the three with an explicit, named training/certification-tracking capability (certification expiry alerts, minor-employee school-schedule tracking) tied to scheduling compliance; Deputy and When I Work's compliance features are limited to labor-law/break/overtime automation rather than staff qualification or training records — and while general industry commentary confirms restaurant operators resent disconnected, unsynced tool stacks, no reviewer was found explicitly complaining about running a separate compliance app alongside these three scheduling products specifically.

### Cited Findings
- **HotSchedules** — "The tracking function for certifications is helpful," and "certification expiry alerts help businesses stay ahead of regulatory changes and maintain compliance"; additionally, "the ability to designate minor team members and track their school schedules is useful for compliance" (i.e., minor labor law / school-hours restrictions) — [SelectHub-sourced review summary](https://www.selecthub.com/p/employee-scheduling-software/hotschedules/)
- **HotSchedules** — Integrates with 10+ third-party tools including Toast POS, Paychex, Aloha EPOS, Focus POS, PAR POS, Revel Systems, SpotOn, Squirrel POS; ships "certified integrations for all major POS and payroll providers—so you can go live in days, not months" (vendor claim) — [Fourth/HotSchedules integration marketing](https://hotschedulec.com/hotschedules-seamless-integration-with-pos-payroll-for-end-to-end-workforce-management/)
- **HotSchedules** — Managers can "configure labor rules to document shift transactions and control labor costs"; predictive scheduling, break/meal management, and certification expiry alerts are grouped together as the compliance feature set — [review/marketing summary](https://www.copliancy.com/employee-training-compliance-for-multi-location-businesses/)
- **Deputy** — No certification/training-record tracking feature was found in sourced material; compliance features center on break reminders, missed-break flags, and overtime alerts (labor-law scheduling compliance, not staff qualification tracking) — [Deputy Time Clock](https://www.deputy.com/features/employee-time-clock)
- **When I Work** — No certification/training-record tracking feature was found in sourced material; compliance scope is limited to overtime alerts and pay-rule/time-clock sync — [When I Work: Geofence Time Clock](https://wheniwork.com/blog/geofence-time-clock)
- **Industry-wide pattern (not vendor-specific)** — Restaurant managers "experience frustration when they piece together different systems for orders, payments, inventory, and reporting that don't communicate with each other"; "the best tools offer compliance monitoring, geofenced time clocks, and integrated payroll, eliminating the 'six tools, zero sync' problem that plagues many restaurant operations" — [industry commentary, restaurant scheduling buyer's guide](https://www.selectsoftwarereviews.com/buyer-guide/best-restaurant-scheduling-software)
- **Industry-wide pattern** — "Managing shift schedules across multiple restaurant locations means juggling dozens of variables: varying labor laws by state, employees who work at different sites, high turnover rates, and the constant challenge of matching staffing levels to demand" — [industry commentary](https://www.workstream.us/blog/shift-scheduling-software-multi-location-restaurants)

### Inferences
- None of the three products combine scheduling with the kind of task/compliance-document workflow VenuRite offers (e.g., HACCP task completion, compliance checklists tied to shifts) — HotSchedules' certification-expiry alerts are the closest analog, but that's a narrow HR-compliance feature (an employee's qualification is or isn't current), not an operational compliance/task-management layer woven into the shift itself.
- This confirms the stated constraint from the brief: the three researched products are workforce-scheduling-first, and the "six tools, zero sync" industry frustration (documented at the general/industry level, not from named reviewers on these three products specifically) is exactly the gap VenuRite's compliance+scheduling combination is positioned to close — but this is an inference from adjacent industry commentary, not a first-party review quote naming Deputy/When I Work/HotSchedules as one of the disconnected tools.

### Gaps
- No first-party review was found where a named reviewer of Deputy, When I Work, or HotSchedules explicitly complained "I have to use a separate app for compliance/HACCP/training alongside this scheduling tool." This is an important gap: the "juggling multiple apps" evidence found is industry-level commentary about restaurant tech stacks in general (POS, inventory, scheduling), not specific first-person complaints about pairing a scheduling app with a compliance app. A report writer should present this as an inferred/likely opportunity, not a confirmed customer pain point, unless further targeted review-mining (e.g., searching HotSchedules App Store reviews for "HACCP" or "food safety" or "checklist") turns up direct evidence — this research did not have tool-call budget to run that narrower search.
- Could not determine whether Deputy or When I Work offer any training-record or certification-tracking module at all (as opposed to simply not being covered in the sources found) — sourced material may be incomplete rather than conclusively confirming absence.
