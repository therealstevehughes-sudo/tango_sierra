The Rota calendar — your single entry point for shifts

Open "Rota month view" from the Roster section of the menu. It shows a month grid with department filter chips above it; tap a day to see that day's shifts and decide on any pending requests. A header icon drills into a per-person week view for more detail, and a "+" icon lets you post a one-off shift without waiting for the weekly template.

Approving a shift someone has claimed

Open the Rota calendar, tap the day, and use the "Approve" button next to that person's name in the day's detail. This turns their shift from claimed (blue) to approved (green) for everyone who can see the calendar.

Assigning a shift directly

From the same day detail in the Rota calendar, tap "Assign" on any open shift and pick who should take it. The system checks the person's certifications for that role before letting the assignment go through, and will tell you if someone is missing a required certificate.

Setting up shift periods

"Shift Periods" lets you split each day into two or three named periods — for example Morning, Afternoon and Evening — with start and end times. Everything downstream, including the calendar views and auto-assign, filters and plans around these periods, so set this up before anything else.

Master Rota Settings — recurring staffing needs

Use "Master Rota Settings" to describe what's needed on a recurring basis — for example, every Friday evening the bar needs three people plus one on standby — by department, role, day of week and period. Once defined, use "generate shifts" for the week you want, and the real shifts are created from that template automatically. This is for repeating patterns; use the Rota calendar's own "+" button for a genuine one-off addition instead.

Fair Auto-Assign

Fair Auto-Assign proposes who should fill a set of open shifts for a chosen week, based on certifications, correct role, no day-off conflicts, no double-booking, and a fairness rule that favours whoever has worked the fewest hours so far. It only ever produces a preview — nothing is committed until you review it and confirm.

Shift Fairness Review

This screen shows how many shifts each person has had, listed strictly alphabetically rather than as a ranked league table — this is deliberate, so the tool can't be used to single anyone out or create unhealthy competition. Use it to spot genuine imbalances, not to rank staff against each other.

Approving or denying a day-off request

Open the Rota calendar, tap the day the request falls on, and you'll see Approve and Deny buttons right alongside that day's shifts. A small amber dot on the calendar itself flags any day that has a pending request waiting for a decision.

Turning the Roster add-on on for your venue

If Roster items in the menu show locked with a padlock icon, the add-on hasn't been enabled for your organisation yet. Tapping a locked item opens an upsell screen with the current price and an enable button.

Staff management

Add, deactivate or edit staff from the Staff Management screen under Settings. Deactivating someone (rather than deleting them) preserves their history while removing their ability to log in.

Departments and shift requirements

Departments group staff and shifts by section (for example Kitchen, Bar, Front of House). Certification requirements can be set per role, per site, in addition to the standard defaults, from the relevant settings screen — these are what Fair Auto-Assign and direct assignment both check before letting an assignment through.

Shift verification queue

If your venue requires clock-in/out photos, any that need a human decision (for example a declined or ambiguous photo) appear in the Shift Verification Queue, reachable from the People section of the menu, for a supervisor or manager to review.

Why the app sometimes shows "you don't have permission"

Some actions (approving shifts, deciding day-off requests, posting shifts, managing staff) are deliberately restricted to venue manager tier and above. If someone who should have access sees a permission error, check their role is actually set to venue manager, regional or executive — a mismatched role is the most common cause.
