Logging in with a PIN

On a shared venue device, tap your own name from the list on the home screen, then enter your PIN. If you can't find your name, ask a manager to check you've been added as active staff at your site. A wrong PIN shows a clear rejection message rather than letting you in as someone else.

Logging in for the first time on a new device

If the staff list is empty or you're not on it yet, look for "Not on this list? Sign in another way" near the bottom of the screen, or ask your manager to add you first.

Logging out versus ending your shift

"Log out" simply signs you out of the device and can be used at any time, including mid-shift, for example when handing the device to a colleague. "End shift" is for when you are actually finishing for the day — it checks whether anything is still outstanding and records your clock-out time. Use "Log out" for a quick handover and "End shift" only when your working day is genuinely done.

Changing the app's language

Tap the globe/language icon — on the login screen it floats near the bottom right of the screen; once you're logged in it sits inside the "more" (three-dot) menu on your home screen. Pick your language from the list that opens. The change applies immediately and is remembered for your account.

My scheduled tasks

"My scheduled tasks" shows the list of checks and jobs assigned to you for today, in order. Work through them one at a time; each one tells you exactly what's required (a reading, a photo, a signature, a yes/no check) before letting you move to the next.

Doing an ad-hoc task

Use "Do an ad-hoc task" for something that needs doing but was never on your scheduled list — for example an unplanned delivery that needs checking, or an off-schedule temperature reading. This is different from "Log something that just happened", which is for reporting a problem rather than completing a task.

Reporting a problem ("Log something that just happened")

Use this when something has gone wrong — a supply problem, a venue problem, or anything else worth flagging — rather than for a routine task. You can mark it "urgent" if it needs attention right away regardless of how long it sits unresolved. You can review anything you've reported under "Things I've reported".

What happens to an issue I raise

It goes to your manager, and will escalate further automatically if it isn't handled within a set time. You can check its current status at any point from "Things I've reported" / "My Raised Issues".

Claiming an open shift

Open "Book shifts and days off" from the menu, or tap the floating calendar button on your home screen if you have one. Tap any day to see the shifts scheduled for it, then claim one that's open. Claiming a shift records you as "claimed" (shown as a blue dot) until a manager approves it (which turns it green).

Requesting a day off

In the same "Book shifts and days off" calendar, use the "book days off instead" toggle, then select the day or days you want and submit. Your request shows as an amber dot while it's pending, and either turns the day grey (approved) or clears back to normal (denied) once a manager decides.

What the coloured dots on the shift calendar mean

Green means your shift is approved. Blue means you've claimed it but a manager hasn't approved it yet. Purple means you're on standby for that shift. Amber means you've requested a day off and it's still pending a decision. A greyed-out day means your day off has been approved.

Why you might not be able to claim a shift

A few reasons are possible: you may be missing a certification required for that role, the shift may already be full, or claiming it would put you over a weekly limit set for that category of shift. If you're not sure which applies, ask your manager.

Cancelling a shift you've already claimed

Open the shift again from the calendar and use the cancel option shown there. If you can't see how, ask a supervisor to remove you from it on your behalf.

Standby shifts

A standby shift means you're on call as a backup for that period rather than guaranteed to work it. It shows with its own colour on the calendar, separate from a confirmed shift.

Shift verification photos

Some venues require a photo when you clock in or out of a shift. This confirms the person clocking in or out is genuinely at the venue — it protects you as much as it protects the business from a shift being logged by someone who isn't actually there. If your photo isn't accepted, try again in better lighting, or ask a supervisor to verify you manually.

Using the app with no internet connection

Anything already loaded on your device — including this help content — keeps working with no internet. Anything that needs to reach the server, such as claiming a shift, submitting a report, or signing in for the very first time on a brand-new device, needs a connection. Reconnect and try again once you're back online; nothing you've already submitted is lost by a temporary drop in connection.

The app looks different from what I remember

The app is updated from time to time, and a browser tab can keep showing an old cached copy even after an update goes live. Fully closing the browser tab or app and reopening it clears this out and loads the current version.
